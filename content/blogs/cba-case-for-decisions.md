## Introduction

Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA) is a foundational tool for analysts and decision-makers looking to drive strategic change through data. From operational improvements to technology investments, CBA helps frame recommendations in terms of impact and trade-offs. This post shares a structured approach to applying CBA in client engagements — particularly useful for junior and mid-level analysts seeking a repeatable method for facilitating decisions.

## The Business Problem

An e-commerce client was facing a challenge with post-onboarding customer retention. Early churn was undermining the return on acquisition spend, and marketing teams needed a more focused strategy for engaging high-value users. While several ideas were floated internally, the client needed a clear, data-backed case to prioritize next steps.

## Step-by-Step CBA Framework

We used a structured framework to guide the analysis and support decision-making:

1. **Define the decision context**
2. **Identify and quantify costs and benefits**
3. **Model expected outcomes**
4. **Compare alternatives**
5. **Communicate a recommendation**

## Applying the Framework

### 1. Define the Decision Context

The core question was: *Is it worthwhile to invest in CLV-based segmentation and cross-tool integration to reduce churn and improve marketing efficiency?*

### 2. Identify Costs and Benefits

We collaborated with stakeholders to scope the potential investment. This included analyst and engineering time, tool integrations, and operational overhead.

On the benefit side, we looked at potential gains in retention, more effective marketing segmentation, and downstream impacts on customer value and revenue efficiency.

### 3. Forecasting and Modeling

To evaluate potential outcomes, we used historical data to build a CLV prediction model and designed marketing segments based on behavior and predicted value. This enabled more tailored engagement strategies.

Models were used to simulate potential business outcomes under different scenarios — including doing nothing, piloting a small segment, or scaling a full solution.

### 4. Scenario Comparison

We compared projected outcomes across multiple scenarios to frame the trade-offs clearly. Each option outlined the relative investment, operational complexity, and business upside — giving decision-makers a practical lens for evaluation.

### 5. Communicating the Case

The final output was a concise recommendation, packaged with visuals and simple assumptions. We emphasized the business logic over technical detail, which helped stakeholders quickly understand the rationale behind the preferred path forward.

## Analyst Takeaways

- Frame the CBA around a clear business question
- Quantify both tangible and intangible benefits
- Keep assumptions transparent and grounded in data
- Use scenario analysis to clarify trade-offs
- Present findings in stakeholder-friendly language

For analysts supporting strategic decisions, CBA offers a disciplined way to connect insights to action. This structured approach helps transform analysis from an academic exercise into a catalyst for change.
